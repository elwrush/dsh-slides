Creative writing · Lesson 1

Six Little Stories

Today you will write five stories of six words, then one of fifty words.

Nothing today is marked.

Nothing you write is corrected or collected.

Levels of thinking

Create sits at the top of the pyramid.

To make something new you first have to remember, understand, apply, analyse and evaluate.

Today you will plan one story before you start writing.

What we are doing today

1

Write five stories.

Each one is six words long, and you have seven minutes.

2

Write one of them out.

It will run to about fifty words, in one paragraph.

3

Read your story out loud.

Each listener tells you one thing they saw.

By the end, you will have written a story and heard it read back.

Six words

Read these three

For sale: baby shoes, never worn.

Class trip. I stayed on the bus.

The dog knew before we did.

The first is usually credited to Ernest Hemingway.

Find all three in the stories above.

1

Something has happened to a person.

2

Something has changed, but not in a good way.

3

Something has been left out. We need to figure out what.

Time to write.

At the end you will choose the best one and make it into a story.

Now we will write fifty words

Three things to put in it

1

One person

Say who they are and what they want.

2

One place

Put all of it in one room.

3

One thing goes wrong

It happens inside the next minute of the story.

The whole thing runs to about fifty words, in one paragraph.

Here is a whole story

I’m not who they say I am. I didn’t do the things they say I did. I was away at the time, in a strange land where everything was fine and beautiful. How could I have killed those people when I have been walking for centuries through an endless garden.

It wasn’t me; I wasn’t there.

Listen to it read aloud.

One story in twelve minutes

Finish the paragraph. Leave the spelling for the editing lesson.

Go.

Reading stories out loud

Read your story out loud

1Listeners close their eyes.

2Read it out loud, slowly.

3Then say nothing at all.

4Each listener says one thing they saw.

The writer cannot see what you saw, so tell them what it was. ‘I liked it’ is not something they can use.

Before you go

Think of a story you would like to write next.

Say it to the person beside you in one sentence.
