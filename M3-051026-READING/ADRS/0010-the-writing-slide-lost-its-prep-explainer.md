# 0010 — The writing slide lost its PREP explainer

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-06
- **Guarded by:** nothing. The line was 24pt body text in a legal position, so every gate passed it; the only check is reading the slide against the plan.

## Context

The writing slide carried a sentence spelling out each move of the frame:

> Position — say what you think. Reason — say why. Evidence — from the text.
> Position — say it again.

**But the PREP frame is already on the slide, above it**, as four tiles reading
*Position · Reason · Evidence · Position*. So the sentence restated, in prose, a
diagram sitting directly above it — [0004](0004-the-deck-was-rebuilt-to-one-idea-per-slide.md)'s
duplication pattern, in the one place the rebuild had left it.

It came from the printed plan, which is where it belongs: Stage 7 tells the teacher
to *"talk it through in one minute: Position, Reason, Example, Position"*. The
**talking** is the teacher's job and the tiles are the slide's — the sentence was
the teacher's script printed next to the diagram it describes.

## Decision

**The sentence is removed.** The slide keeps the four PREP tiles, the word count
("at least 70 words"), and the instruction to refer to the article. Nothing else
changed.

## Consequences

- Measured after the change: the slide still contains *Position*, *Reason*,
  *Evidence* and *at least 70 words*, and no longer contains *"say what you think"*.
- One fewer block on a slide that was also carrying a heading, a full-width tile
  row, a task sentence, a reference instruction and a countdown timer.
- The teacher's explanation is unchanged in the plan, which is the document read
  while running the lesson.

## What breaks if you change this

- **Put the explainer back "so students know what each move means".** The tiles are
  the explanation and they are already there; adding the prose back restores the
  duplication and the slide's crowding.
- **Remove the tiles instead, keeping the sentence.** The reverse trade is worse:
  four words in a row read at a glance, a 24-word sentence does not.
